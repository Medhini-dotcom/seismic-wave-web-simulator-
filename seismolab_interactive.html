Seismology field notes

Seismology studies earthquakes and the waves they generate. Seismograms record ground motion and help scientists estimate earthquake location, depth and size. USGS: The Science of Earthquakes ↗

P-waves (Primary)

Compressional body waves that move particles roughly parallel to the direction of travel. They are generally the first seismic arrivals.

S-waves (Secondary)

Shear body waves that move particles perpendicular to propagation. They arrive after P-waves and do not travel through fluids.

Surface waves

Travel along or near Earth's surface and can produce prolonged ground motion. Their effects depend on source, path and local geology.

Epicenter & hypocenter

The hypocenter (focus) is the point where rupture begins within Earth; the epicenter is its position projected onto the surface.

Magnitude & intensity

Magnitude describes earthquake size, while shaking intensity describes effects at a particular place. Intensity can vary from one location to another for the same event. Learn more ↗
